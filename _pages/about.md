<h1>Welcome to my page!</h1>

My name is Jabin Binnendyk, and I am currently completing my PhD at Cornell University under the supervision of <a href="https://gordonpennycook.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Gordon Pennycook</a>. I anticipate completing my PhD in April 2027 and will be on the academic job market starting in May 2027!



This website contains information about my academic work, research, and life outside of academia. If you have any questions, feel free to get in touch!

--- 
**Research interests**: Overconfidence, Metacognition, Judgment and decision making, Calibration, Self-censorship, Social influence, Social cognition, Collective judgment, 

Generally speaking, my research examines miscalibration—how accurately people understand themselves and their social worlds—and how these perceptions shape their judgments, decisions, and social interactions.

These interests have led me to research overconfidence and self-censorship, two phenomena that illustrate these misperceptions.

**Featured Publications**:

**Binnendyk, J.**, Li, S., Costello, T., Hale, R., Moore, D. A., & Pennycook, G. (2025).
<a href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/09567976251401538?casa_token=nL7gvE-DoQUAAAAA:QCA4EaR_wQf3qWQ63ljTa665LwwZMkHaxK9tVXbDpNckd-LlHL82OvwF1TMkeWGUor85l4nd5xyD" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Is overconfidence a trait? An adversarial collaboration</a>.
*Psychological Science* 
* In this paper, we examined whether conceptual overconfidence across different tasks (from guessing the suits of randomly drawn cards to predicting whether someone has an innie or outtie bellybutton) was related, suggesting a general underlying trait. We found that, regardless of the task, people who were overconfident on one task were more likely to be overconfident on another. 

*This paper received the Significant Paper Award: Cornell University Department of Psychology.*

**Binnendyk, J.**, & Pennycook, G. (2024). <a href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/judgment-and-decision-making/article/individual-differences-in-overconfidence-a-new-measurement-approach/D2CC80B7DBFBF152F19356F337F0A2AF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Individual differences in overconfidence: A new measurement approach.</a> *Judgment and Decision Making*


* We challenge traditional methods for measuring overconfidence and propose a novel approach called the Generalized Overconfidence Task (<a href="https://github.com/Jabinnendyk/GOT" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">GOT</a>). The GOT addresses a key limitation of traditional measures: estimates of overconfidence are confounded with differences in ability. Using this approach, we demonstrate that individual differences in overconfidence emerge once you account for differences in ability.*
